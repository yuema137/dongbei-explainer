# Semantic anti-patterns

- A definition whose key words merely restate the term.
- “X enables Y through Z” without actors, changed steps, or consequences.
- A metaphor that takes longer to learn than the mechanism.
- A plausible invented causal story.
- Saying a tool “solves” a problem when it moves, narrows, or amortizes it.
- Removing edge conditions, equations, identity changes, or resource costs to sound simple.
- Treating a knowledgeable reader as a beginner because they asked for clarity.
- Printing the full explanation checklist as headings for a small question.

Reject an intuitive sentence if it cannot be mapped back to the real mechanism.
